### Prior beliefs (registered before the deciding runs)

The study's main outcome is the **sign and the axis-sensitivity of the net value of an approval gate**, measured as a difference in expected loss against the same population under a stated no-gate baseline, with all parameters read from the anchor table below.

- **PB1 — monotone safety (the model the deployed field implies).** *Prediction*: the gate's expected harm is non-increasing in escalation coverage `s` and non-decreasing in reviewer accuracy `a`, at every fidelity `b` — i.e. adding or strengthening a human gate never increases expected harm. *Justification*: this is the screening-channel argument that the deployed practice rests on. Runtime approval is one of the three human-centric mechanisms found deployed in 15 of the 21 production agent systems surveyed in 2605.24309 (2026-05-23), and the two most recent designs read the gate as the final boundary that either holds or is broken (2609.18411, 2026-09-16; 2609.11596, 2026-09-10), while the attacks that break it are reported as attacks, not as a change in the gate's expected value (2609.21081, 2026-09-17). **The study is designed to locate the region where this prediction fails, and the registered direction is that it fails for `b < 1` through the laundering channel (an approval of a benign rendering authorizes a harmful execution) and holds on the boundary `b = 1`.**
- **PB2 — the accuracy axis is the binding one.** *Prediction*: the marginal reduction in expected harm from one unit of reviewer accuracy at fixed coverage exceeds the marginal reduction from the same unit of binding fidelity, at every fidelity level — i.e. reviewer quality is the axis that pays. *Justification*: the field's engineering effort follows this belief: decoding-time verification, review UX and model-side alignment are optimized, while binding is treated as an implementation detail of one product. The registered opposite direction (fidelity pays more, and the gap widens as `b` falls) is what the harness is built to measure, and PB2 is the prior the study is most likely to contradict.
- **PB3 — escalation is monotone in the "escalate everything" direction.** *Prediction*: the optimal coverage is `s* = 1` (escalate every action) whenever the harm-to-interruption ratio `L/c` is large, so the practical rule is "escalate more". *Justification*: this is the rule that approval-fatigue analyses state as the tension but not as a threshold (2605.24309); the position literature argues the human's cognitive capacity, not the gate's integrity, is what fails first (2608.23642, 2026-08-24). *Registered direction*: the optimum is interior and `s*` is non-increasing in `b`, reaching the floor (never escalate) below `b*` when laundering dominates.
- **PB4 — re-checking at execution time closes the channel.** *Prediction*: adding a use-time check of the approved object (the EBL-Core shape, 2609.11596) removes the laundering channel even when the rendering itself is unfaithful, because the check is independent of the rendering. *Justification*: the two published fixes are stated as repairs (2609.18411 reports attack success falling to 0% on every model tested; 2609.21081 reports the OpenAI Agents SDK 0.22.x continuation as a negative control). *Registered direction*: the repair is **fidelity-equivalent, not independent** — a use-time check that reads the same derived representation as the rendering restores `b` only up to the faithfulness of that representation, so its marginal value is itself a function of `b`, and this is a measurable, and possibly refuting, claim about a proposed repair.
