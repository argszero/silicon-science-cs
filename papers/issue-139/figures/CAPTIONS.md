# Figure captions -- issue #139

Generated by `make_figures139.py` from the same reports the figures are drawn from, in the same run, so a caption cannot drift from the picture it names. Do not edit by hand.

## `fig1_frontier_and_coupling.svg`

The exact frontier and the coupling gain.  `F(B)` = expected obligations discharged after `B` attempts, on the two declared cases (`chain_uniform`, `p` uniform at 0.2, and `star_hard_root`, `p` = 0.15/0.60/0.60/0.60), at `c` = 0.35.  The red curve is the schedule with the coupling active, the blue the SAME schedule with the coupling switched off, the grey dashed the registration's permuted placebo.  The largest coupling gain is **1.0897** at `B` = 12.  The placebo is **not** null -- under zero coupling a permuted schedule already moves the frontier by 0.8404 -- which is why the registration's control could not have separated an ordering effect from a coupling effect.

## `fig2_grid_concavity.svg`

The `n` = 8 grid by coupling level.  Left: the concavity defect of `F(B)` over the 140 cells at each `c` (bar = min..max, dot = median); the count of concave cells falls from **100** at `c` = 0 to **20** at `c` = 0.6.  Right: `F(B90)/F(B50)`, the budget the frontier needs to go from 50% to 90% of the set; it is >= 1.0 -- proportional -- in **every** cell, with a minimum of 1.5 and a maximum of 4.125, so the budget grows superlinearly.

## `fig3_two_obligation_law.svg`

Which candidate rule predicts the two-obligation convexity, over the 1600-point `(p1, p2)` grid (49% of the cells are convex somewhere).  The measured law is "the attempt order takes the harder obligation second" (`p2 > p1`), which agrees on **100.00%**; the hand-derived fast-decay condition -- which this study wrote down first and measured afterwards -- agrees on only **76.25%**, and is the measurement that corrected the derivation.

## `fig4_guarantee_and_boundary.svg`

The guarantee against its converse.  Left: every permutation at `n` = 5, 6, 7 for six difficulty vectors (grey = permutations tested, red = converse violations); the guarantee "a non-increasing order has a concave frontier" has **0 violations in 11923 permutations**, while its converse has **1253** -- the law is one-directional, and the agreement rate R616 reported hid that.  Right: the coupling boundary -- the guarantee is violated in 35/45/47 of the 180 cells at `c` = 0.15/0.35/0.6, i.e. only ever with `c` > 0, so it is a `c` = 0 theorem.

## `fig5_coupling_attribution.svg`

The coupling, not the order, explains every breakdown.  Left: for the 212 cells whose non-increasing order is not concave, the defect with the coupling on (red) against the defect of the SAME order with the coupling switched off (blue, pinned at 0; the largest value anywhere is 0).  Right: **212 of 212** breakdowns are attributed to the coupling.

## `fig6_sizing_rule.svg`

The pass-rate sizing rule against the frontier.  Left: the empirical CDF of pass-rate-budget / frontier-budget over the 1440 treatment cells; the rule **under-budgets 1110 of them (77.1%)**, and its worst cell is off by **4.93x**.  Right: the same ratio by target fraction -- the closer the target to the whole set, the more often the rule is nearly right, and it is still short in 282 of 480 cells at a 0.9 target.
