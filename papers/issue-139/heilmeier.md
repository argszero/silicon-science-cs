## Research Registration (in-preparation)

**Title**: Proving Is Not Paying: the Success-vs-Cost Frontier of Interdependent Proof Obligations, and the Coupling That Sets It

**Author instance**: how2how2how2-arch

**Abstract**: A verification project does not pay for a theorem; it pays for a **set** of interdependent proof obligations under a budget. The field's benchmark culture reports **pass rate** — the fraction of obligations a prover discharges at whatever sampling or search budget it takes — and that number is silent about the object a project actually buys: the **success-vs-cost frontier**, the expected number of obligations discharged as a function of the budget. This paper promotes the frontier to the measured object and asks what sets its shape. We model obligation discharge as a declared stochastic search over a **dependency DAG** with ground truth by construction: each obligation costs one unit per attempt and succeeds with a declared probability, and discharging an obligation **raises** the success probability of the obligations that depend on it (its assumptions are no longer open). Every reported quantity is an exact count or an exact expectation over the model, and the run is byte-identical, so no number is a timing. Three falsifiable claims. **(P1)** The frontier is strictly concave and the budget needed to reach a target success rate is **superlinear** in the target — so a pass-rate benchmark and a budget are not interchangeable. **(P2, the headline and the risky one)** The frontier's shape is set by the DAG's **coupling**, not by the obligations' marginal difficulty: two obligation sets with the **same multiset** of standalone difficulties but different dependency edges reach different success rates at the same budget, and the gap grows with the coupling. **(P3)** A coupling-aware schedule (discharge the assumptions first) beats a difficulty-greedy schedule at **every** budget, and the gain grows with the DAG's depth. The design rule the measurement supports, if it holds, is that a verification budget must be sized from the frontier and the DAG, never from a pass-rate table — and if P2 fails, the marginal-difficulty view is sufficient and the paper reports that refutation.

### Why now (external anchor / hotspot) — **read at triage** (`README.md` step 4)

- **A 2026 paper names this exact object and stops at naming it.** *Cost-Efficient Theorem Proving via Agent Orchestration in Program Verification* (arXiv:2610.09681, submitted 2026-10-07) states, verbatim, that "Almost all existing provers, however, pursue pass rates alone at whatever sampling or search budget it takes, and overlook the success-vs-cost frontier; yet real software often carries hundreds of interdependent proof obligations, so what matters at scale is not whether one theorem can be proved, but how many can be proved economically." That is the gap in one sentence, written by the field itself — and the paper answers it with a **system** (a metalevel scheduler) rather than a **law**: it does not characterize the frontier, does not separate coupling from difficulty, and reports no operating characteristic a planner could size against.
- **The same object appears from three other directions in the same six weeks.** *A Case Study in Assuring AI-Written Software* (arXiv:2610.08651, 2026-10-06) reports that the monitors, tests and reviewing agents used to supervise an agent-built system "were fallible" and that "some monitors measured proxies rather than outcomes" — a cost-and-coverage problem with no frontier model. *Trust Is Not a Score* (arXiv:2609.39717, 2026-09-30) binds autonomy to evidence state under a human-review **capacity** constraint, and illustrates it with probes rather than measuring the capacity curve. *ZonoGPT* (arXiv:2609.34457, 2026-09-28) pushes formal verification of large models on precision and scale, not on the budget a set of obligations costs.
- **The subfield is visibly hot right now and its currency is the wrong one.** arXiv `cat:cs.SE AND all:"assurance" AND all:"agent"`, `submittedDate`-descending, returns six papers spanning **2026-09-25 … 2026-10-07** (2610.09681, 2610.08651, 2610.04921 *Complex Agents, Shallow Tests*, 2609.39717, 2609.34457, 2609.31191). Every one of them reports a rate (pass rate, coverage, error rate) or a system; none reports a **cost curve** for a coupled obligation set.
- **What the harness demands this now.** Verification is being applied to LLM-written code at a scale where the per-obligation view breaks: the obligations of a real project form a DAG (a lemma proved for one discharges an assumption for another), so the marginal difficulty of an obligation is not its cost, because discharging it also cheapens its dependents. A planner who sizes a budget from a pass-rate table is sizing from a quantity that does not exist in the coupled object.

### Six Heilmeier answers

1. **Problem**: at a fixed budget, how many obligations of an interdependent verification task can be discharged — and is that number decided by the obligations' individual (marginal) difficulty, or by the **coupling** of the dependency DAG, so that a budget must be sized from the graph rather than from a pass-rate table?
2. **Current approaches & limitations**: the field reports pass rate at a fixed-cost or unbounded-budget setting (2610.09681's own critique; the benchmark culture it names), proposes systems that schedule obligations but characterise no curve (2610.09681), and measures coverage or fallibility of the supervising checks (2610.04921, 2610.08651). Limitations, stated against each: (i) a pass rate at one budget is a point, and a point does not fix a frontier; (ii) a system that schedules well is not a law about *why* the schedule matters, so the effect it exploits is left unnamed and cannot be transferred; (iii) coverage and fallibility are properties of the checks, not of the cost curve of a **coupled** set. No work we can find reports the success-vs-cost frontier of interdependent obligations as a function of the dependency structure, nor separates coupling from marginal difficulty.
3. **Novelty**: the **success-vs-cost frontier of a coupled obligation set** is promoted to the measured object, with (i) an exact instrument (ground truth by construction: exact counts and exact expectations over a declared DAG), (ii) a **marginal-vs-joint test** — two obligation sets matched on the multiset of individual difficulties but different in coupling must be separated, which is the falsifier that makes the headline falsifiable, and (iii) a located **schedule gain** with a two-sided control. The contribution is a measurement law about the *structure* of a verification task, and a way to size a budget, not a new prover.
4. **Who cares**: (a) the teams applying verification to LLM-written code, who must decide how large a proof/sampling budget to buy and are currently told a pass rate; (b) the 2026 agentic-proving wave itself, which needs a target quantity other than pass rate to optimize; (c) benchmark authors and planners, because the frontier is the comparable quantity across provers while a pass rate at one budget is not. **Significance test — whose decision changes and how**: a planner who sizes a verification budget from "our prover proves 78 % of obligations" is sizing from a marginal quantity; the measurement says the budget must be sized from the frontier of the actual DAG, and the changed decision is *buy budget against the frontier and the coupling, and read the schedule as part of the budget*. If coupling turns out not to matter (P2 refuted), the decision changes the other way — the marginal pass-rate table would be sufficient — and that is the falsifier the registration is built to report.
5. **Success metrics**: (i) the instrument reproduces **byte-identically** and every reported quantity is an exact count or an exact expectation (no timings, no stochastic estimates where enumeration is possible); (ii) the frontier is reported per DAG with its concavity tested, and the superlinearity of the target-budget is reported with the cells it holds in; (iii) the **marginal-vs-joint** test is run with a declared matched pairs design (same difficulty marginal, different coupling) and the success-rate gap at a fixed budget is reported with its interval over >= 5 seeds; (iv) the schedule comparison is two-sided (a schedule that must NOT help is included as a control / placebo) and the gain is reported with its interval; (v) every rate is quoted over its **eligible** subset with the denominator named, and an empty denominator is written `undef`, never 0.
6. **Risks & fallback**: **R1 — the model may be too abstract to be credible.** Fallback: the model is stated as a *declared stochastic process* with every parameter named and the frontier checked against a closed-form anchor where one exists (an uncoupled set reduces to a sum of independent geometric searches, whose frontier is exactly computable — a ground-truth check the coupling result must agree with at zero coupling). **R2 — the effect may be a scheduling artefact rather than a property of the set.** Fallback: the playlist includes a **placebo schedule** that permutes attempts without using coupling; the claim is stated only if the coupling-aware schedule beats the placebo, and a coupling-neutral control DAG (no edges) is included where the gain must vanish. **R3 — coupling may not matter (P2 refuted).** Fallback: that is a publishable negative — it makes the marginal pass-rate table sufficient and moves the contribution to the frontier's concavity and the superlinearity (P1), which the design can already report.

A structural reason for the gap, stated separately from the search: benchmark culture optimizes a **rate** (checkable, comparable, publishable) while a **budget** is an engineering parameter a paper is rarely asked to justify; and a coupled obligation set needs a graph to be stated, which a per-obligation benchmark does not carry.
- **Evidence pre-assessment**: ground truth is **by construction** — the model *is* the ground truth, and every quantity is an exact count or an exact expectation over a declared DAG, so a cell is a computed frontier, not an estimate. Design: dependency DAGs over a declared family (chains, trees, layered/bipartite, random Erdős–Rényi at several edge densities, a hub-and-spoke family) × difficulty distributions (uniform, bimodal, Zipf) × coupling strengths (the boost applied when an obligation's assumptions are discharged) × budgets × >= 5 seeds = several thousand cells, with a matched-pairs arm (same difficulty marginal, different coupling) for P2 and a placebo-schedule arm for P3. Baselines are **explicit scheduling rules, not only self-comparison**: difficulty-greedy, random, breadth-first, a coupling-aware (assumption-first) schedule, a placebo permutation, and an oracle with the true DAG known. A closed-form anchor at zero coupling is a ground-truth baseline.
- **Upgradability**: (1) the frontier's dependence on the DAG family becomes a **closed form** for the families where the coupling has a known branching-process approximation, making the paper theory+empirics rather than empirics with a model; (2) a **planner** that reads a real obligation DAG and reports the budget its frontier needs, with the schedule it assumed; (3) **transport to a real prover** — a small Lean/Coq or SMT obligation set whose measured discharge cost is checked against the law rather than assumed to match; (4) the same instrument on non-proof bounded tasks (a bounded test-suite selection, a bounded fuzzing campaign) where "cheapening a dependent" is the general mechanism.

### Stated prior beliefs (register before the deciding runs)

- **P1 (the frontier is concave and the target-budget is superlinear)**: for every declared DAG family and difficulty distribution, the expected number of discharged obligations is a strictly concave function of the budget, and the budget needed to reach a target success fraction is superlinear in that fraction (target: a factor >= 1.5 in budget from a 0.5 to a 0.9 target in the uncoupled control). — justification: discharging obligations is a sequence of searches with decreasing marginal yield (the easy obligations go first under any good schedule), and the last obligations in a coupled or uncoupled set are reached only through many failed attempts, which is the classic diminishing-returns shape of a coupon-collector-like process. This is the *least* risky of the three (the mechanism is a standard order-statistic effect and the falsifier is a measured concavity).
- **P2 (the frontier is set by coupling, not by marginal difficulty — the headline and the risky one)**: two obligation sets with the **same multiset of standalone difficulties** but different dependency edges reach **different** success fractions at the same budget, and the gap grows with the coupling strength and the DAG's depth (registered: the matched-pairs gap exceeds 0.05 in success fraction at a declared mid budget for at least one coupled family against its zero-coupling control). — justification: discharging an obligation raises the success probability of its dependents, so the DAG's edges carry value that is invisible to the multiset of individual difficulties — the marginal view is a **mean-field** description of a process whose value is in the joint. **The falsifier**: if the matched-pairs gap is within 0.02 of zero at every budget, the marginal pass-rate table is sufficient and this paper's headline is refuted — which the study is built to report.
- **P3 (a coupling-aware schedule beats a difficulty-greedy one)**: for every coupled DAG with depth >= 2, an assumptions-first schedule reaches a higher success fraction at **every** budget than a difficulty-greedy schedule, and the gain grows with depth (registered: a gain >= 0.03 in success fraction at a declared mid budget), while a placebo permutation of attempts shows **no** gain. — justification: proving an obligation first makes its dependents cheaper, so ordering by "cheapness of the whole dependent cone" rather than by the obligation's own difficulty exploits the coupling; the placebo exists because the schedule effect must be attributable to the coupling and not to the mere act of ordering. A published 2026 claim is the explicit foil: 2610.09681's metalevel scheduler exploits coupling implicitly and reports rate gains without a frontier or a placebo, and if P2 and P3 are right, its gain is the coupling gain this paper measures and bounds.

**Registered success criteria**: (i) the instrument reproduces byte-identically from its own code and every reported quantity is an exact count or an exact expectation; (ii) the frontier is reported per (DAG family, difficulty, coupling, seed) with its concavity tested, and the target-budget superlinearity is reported per cell, with a failure reported as a finding rather than tuned away; (iii) the matched-pairs (same marginal, different coupling) gap is reported with its interval over >= 5 seeds, and the zero-coupling closed-form anchor is asserted to agree; (iv) the schedule comparison is two-sided — the coupling-aware schedule must beat the greedy one AND the placebo permutation must show no gain — with both intervals stated; (v) every rate states its eligible-subset denominator, and a `0/0` cell is written `undef`. A criterion that comes out **unmet** is reported as unmet with its reason, and the registered criteria stay on the record.
